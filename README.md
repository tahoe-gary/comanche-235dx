# Comanche 235DX

### A Jet-A future for the Piper Comanche

**Comanche 235DX** is an independent feasibility project exploring the installation of the **[DeltaHawk DHK235](https://deltahawk.com/)** Jet-A piston engine in the **[Piper PA-24-250 and PA-24-260-series Comanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_PA-24_Comanche)**.

The idea is simple:

> Take one of general aviation's most aerodynamically efficient legacy airframes and give it a modern, fuel-efficient, high-altitude-capable powerplant.

The ultimate goal is to determine whether a commercially viable Supplemental Type Certificate (STC) could be developed for the conversion.

This project is exploratory and is **not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by Piper Aircraft or DeltaHawk Engines**.

---

## Why the Comanche?

The Piper Comanche was remarkably advanced when introduced in the 1950s.

Its combination of:

* retractable landing gear
* relatively small frontal area
* efficient wing
* clean aerodynamics
* four-place cabin
* useful payload
* excellent range
* strong cruise performance

still compares favorably with many much newer aircraft.

More than 2,500 PA-24-250s were originally produced, followed by roughly another 1,000 aircraft in the 260 family.

A substantial fleet remains in service today, supported by an active owner community and an established ecosystem of parts, maintenance expertise and existing STCs.

Rather than designing another million-dollar piston single from scratch, the 235DX concept asks:

**What if we modernized the propulsion system of the airplane we already have?**

---

# Why the DeltaHawk DHK235?

The original PA-24-250 is powered by a normally aspirated 250 hp Lycoming O-540. Later Comanche 260 variants use 260 hp O/IO-540 derivatives.

These are proven engines, but the fundamental architecture dates from the 1950s.

The DeltaHawk DHK235 represents a very different approach.

|                 | Lycoming O/IO-540        | DeltaHawk DHK235         |
| --------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Configuration   | Horizontally opposed six | Inverted V4              |
| Cycle           | Four-stroke              | Two-stroke               |
| Displacement    | ~540 cu in               | 202 cu in / 3.3 L        |
| Rated power     | 250–260 hp               | 235 hp                   |
| Fuel            | 100LL                    | Jet-A / Jet-A1           |
| Aspiration      | Normally aspirated*      | Compound boosted         |
| Cooling         | Air                      | Liquid                   |
| Prop drive      | Direct                   | Direct                   |
| Mixture control | Manual                   | Automatic engine control |
| Ignition        | Spark / magnetos         | Compression ignition     |

* Excluding the relatively rare turbocharged Comanche variants.

The DHK235 produces **235 hp maximum continuous power at 2,600 RPM**.

At its published best-economy setting it produces approximately **188 hp at 2,200 RPM while consuming 10.2 gallons per hour of Jet-A**.

---

# The High-Altitude Advantage

Fuel economy is only part of the idea.

The more interesting difference is **available horsepower at altitude**.

A normally aspirated O-540 progressively loses available power as altitude increases.

The DHK235 uses a compound-boosting system incorporating a mechanically driven supercharger, turbocharger and charge-air cooling.

DeltaHawk specifies a critical altitude above approximately 17,500 feet for the engine family.

That creates an intriguing combination with the aerodynamically clean Comanche airframe.

Instead of merely burning less fuel at the same altitude, the 235DX could potentially operate efficiently in the mid-to-high teens while still producing substantial cruise power.

One of the primary goals of this project is therefore to model and eventually determine experimentally:

**How fast can a PA-24 fly at altitude at the DHK235's best-economy setting — approximately 188 hp while burning roughly 10 gallons per hour of Jet-A?**

# Potential Advantages

A successful 235DX conversion could offer:

### Jet-A

Jet-A is widely available worldwide and avoids dependence on leaded aviation gasoline.

### Reduced fuel consumption

The DHK235's published best-economy operating point is approximately:

**188 hp / 10.2 gph Jet-A**

### Altitude power

Compound boosting allows the engine to maintain substantially more power at altitude than the original normally aspirated engine.

### Maximum continuous power

The DHK235 is rated for its full **235 hp continuously**, rather than limiting maximum power to takeoff operation.

### Single-lever operation

No manual mixture control or conventional leaning procedure.

### Liquid cooling

More consistent engine temperatures and the possibility of coolant-based cabin heating rather than an exhaust heat exchanger.

### Mechanical simplicity

The engine eliminates several components associated with conventional aircraft gasoline engines, including:

* magnetos
* spark plugs
* conventional valve train

It also drives the propeller directly without a reduction gearbox.

### Compact architecture

The 3.3-liter inverted-V4 is substantially smaller in frontal area than a 540-cubic-inch horizontally opposed six.

A purpose-designed cowling may therefore offer opportunities for improved cooling aerodynamics and reduced drag.

# Weight: The First Big Question

The DHK235 engine itself is listed at approximately **335 lb**, including starter, alternator, turbocharger and exhaust.

That number does **not**, by itself, answer whether the conversion is lighter than the original Comanche installation.

A complete installation also requires:

* heat exchanger/radiator
* coolant
* coolant plumbing
* charge-air cooling
* ducting
* engine mount
* propeller and spinner
* fuel-system components
* cowling and associated hardware

Fortunately, the PA-24-250 was already designed around the relatively heavy six-cylinder O-540.

One of the first engineering tasks of this project will therefore be to compare:

**complete O-540 firewall-forward weight and moment**

versus

**complete DHK235 firewall-forward weight and moment.**

The goal would be **no ballast and minimal or no loss of useful load**.

Until actual installation weights are available, no claim is being made that the DeltaHawk installation will be lighter.

# Preliminary Performance Targets

These are **engineering targets, not demonstrated performance claims**.

| Parameter                |   Initial Target |
| ------------------------ | ---------------: |
| Maximum continuous power |           235 hp |
| Economy cruise power     |          ~188 hp |
| Economy fuel consumption |  ~10.2 gph Jet-A |
| Target cruise altitude   | 12,000–18,000 ft |
| Target economy cruise    |    175–190+ KTAS |
| High-power cruise        |              TBD |
| Target range             |        1,000+ nm |
| Seats                    |                4 |
| Pressurization           |               No |

One particularly interesting question is whether a clean PA-24 with 235 hp available in the high teens could exceed **200 KTAS**.

That is currently a hypothesis to investigate—not a performance claim.

# Proposed Development Path

## Phase 1 — Feasibility

Before modifying an aircraft:

1. Engage DeltaHawk regarding technical feasibility and support.
2. Identify an experienced Part 23/STC certification partner.
3. Obtain accurate DHK235 installation dimensions and weights.
4. Build an initial PA-24 weight-and-balance model.
5. Model engine mount and cowling geometry.
6. Develop a preliminary cooling-system architecture.
7. Model expected performance versus altitude.
8. Estimate STC development cost and schedule.
9. Survey potential owner demand.
10. Determine expected conversion pricing.

## Phase 2 — Development Aircraft

Acquire a structurally sound PA-24-250 development aircraft.

Before removing the original powerplant:

* obtain an accurate empty weight and CG
* document baseline performance
* instrument the aircraft as appropriate
* document the existing firewall-forward installation
* weigh removed components individually

A low-time O-540 and serviceable propeller could subsequently be sold to offset development-aircraft cost.

Cosmetic restoration would be deliberately deferred until major development work is complete.

## Phase 3 — Prototype Installation

Develop:

* engine mount
* propeller installation
* cooling system
* cowling
* intercooler/charge-air ducting
* fuel system
* engine controls
* electrical integration
* instrumentation

Ground testing would precede flight testing.

## Phase 4 — Certification

An experienced certification organization would lead the FAA approval program, including:

* certification planning
* engineering analysis
* structural substantiation
* conformity
* ground testing
* flight testing
* FAA coordination
* Aircraft Flight Manual Supplement
* Instructions for Continued Airworthiness
* STC approval

The objective would be a **multiple-aircraft STC**, not merely approval of one experimental installation.

# Potential Commercial Product

If technically and economically viable, the project could ultimately support two products.

## 235DX Firewall-Forward Conversion

A conversion package for existing PA-24 owners containing the engine and all required installation components.

## 235DX Remanufactured Aircraft

A complete restoration program using structurally sound legacy Comanche airframes.

A turnkey aircraft might include:

* DHK235 propulsion system
* overhauled landing gear
* restored flight controls
* renewed electrical wiring
* modern Garmin avionics
* digital autopilot
* modern engine instrumentation
* new windows
* LED lighting
* renewed interior
* new paint
* detailed corrosion inspection and airframe restoration

The result would remain a certificated Piper PA-24, but with a substantially modernized propulsion system and aircraft systems.

# Questions This Project Needs to Answer

The purpose of this repository is to answer questions rather than assume the answers.

Among them:

**Engineering**

* What is the actual installed DHK235 firewall-forward weight?
* Where does the resulting aircraft CG fall?
* Can the installation avoid ballast?
* Where should the heat exchanger be located?
* How much cooling drag will the installation introduce?
* What propeller diameter and blade configuration are optimal?
* Can a new cowling reduce total aircraft drag?
* What structural changes are required for the engine mount?
* Are the existing fuel tanks and fuel-system components compatible with Jet-A, or must they be replaced?

**Performance**

* Where do the O-540 and DHK235 available-power curves cross?
* What cruise speed should 188 hp produce at 12–18,000 ft?
* What does 235 hp maximum-continuous cruise look like?
* What climb performance can be expected?
* What is the practical range with standard and auxiliary tanks?

**Certification**

* What certification basis would apply?
* What testing would the FAA require?
* Can one STC reasonably cover the 250, 260, 260B and 260C?
* What would certification cost?
* Who should own and maintain the STC?

**Business**

* How many surviving PA-24-250/260 aircraft are realistic conversion candidates?
* What percentage of owners would consider converting?
* What firewall-forward price would the market support?
* Does a turnkey remanufactured-aircraft business make economic sense?
